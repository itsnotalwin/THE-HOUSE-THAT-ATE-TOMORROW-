# 04 — STORY, CAST, DIALOGUE AND ENDINGS

## Narrative thesis

An unfinished possibility can still matter. The mansion isn't automatically good because it's strange, nor is the Auditor evil because he enforces rules; the emotional truth emerges from the relationships and choices built inside the house. The main thread should remain a playful mystery rather than a lecture or a private autobiographical reconstruction.

## Cast bible

### The Visitor — protagonist
A curious outsider, cautious but willing to engage, often the only character who thinks anything happening is odd. Player is not asked to pick a romantic path, disclose identity or make irreversible early decisions. The Visitor's most distinctive trait is noticing small details other people take for granted.

**Voice:** dry, empathetic, occasionally exasperated. **Arc:** from accidental recruit to active advocate for a place they didn't believe in. **Forbidden:** sarcastic cruelty as a default, explicit promises of romance, private references not approved for public code.

### Fig — cat / receptionist
Black cat wearing a tiny tie. Self-important, acerbic, efficient in ways that make no sense. Keeps guestbook, gives hints, knows more than he admits. Secretly worries that if the house is closed there will be nowhere left to file his paperwork or keep his friends.

**Voice:** concise, formal; tiny bursts of vulnerable feeling. **Arc:** stops hiding behind procedures and speaks at the hearing. **Visual tells:** tail flicks when lying, ears flatten when the Auditor is mentioned, relaxing after the final choice.

### Miss Thimble — ghost librarian
Polite ghost, glasses, floating book, utterly punctilious about order. Feels ashamed that she never finished the book she wanted to write. Her unfinished manuscript proves that unrealized stories can still affect living people.

**Voice:** elegant, gentle, fussy. **Arc:** stops apologizing for being unfinished; joins the proof that the mansion is a lived-in home. **Visual tells:** spectral pages orbit when excited.

### Sir Crumb — mouse knight
Tiny, fierce, imaginative. Afraid of a vegetable and determined to keep his title. He isn't a pure joke: during the final act he chooses to stand by Mabel despite his fear.

**Voice:** dramatic declarations punctured by small honesty. **Arc:** bravery becomes care for someone rather than performing heroism. **Visual tells:** helmet slips down over eyes, toy needle held upright.

### Mabel — caretaker
Kind but evasive. Made a desperate, harmful decision: freezing tomorrow to stop the Auditor. Believes sheltering the house justifies bending time, but sees the cost when residents fade. No justification that erases consequences.

**Voice:** domestic comfort masking precision. **Arc:** admits the choice, trusts the Visitor and lets time resume. **Visual tells:** steady hands making tea; noticeably still when asked about clock.

### The Auditor — bureaucratic antagonist
Tall, immaculate and not a cackling villain. He believes unstable forgotten realities are unsafe and can point to the clock stoppage as valid evidence. There is a lawful way to protect a home that meets the conditions; he initially doesn't believe this house does.

**Voice:** courteous, precise, unsettlingly literal. **Arc:** accepts documented reality and grants an exception if persuaded, whether he likes it or not. **Visual tells:** papers appear from nowhere, shoes always perfectly placed.

## Complete narrative beat sheet

### Prologue: The Letter (2 min)
- Cold open: rain, empty street, letter arrives or is noticed in mailbox, address appears and erases itself.
- First input moves the Visitor; first inspect reveals unusual letter.
- Fig sits at mansion threshold and insists it's a municipal building.
- **Mystery 1:** Why is tomorrow missing? **Payoff later:** Mabel deliberately sealed it to delay demolition.

### Act I: New guest (8–12 min)
- Fig admits the house is invisible to people who don't need it; says the Guestbook won't accept a normal name.
- Mirror amulet unlocks The Almost. It reveals writing on a portrait: THE VISITOR.
- Guestbook puzzle solved; building acknowledges presence, lights bloom, red erasure notice arrives.
- Fig deflects fear with bureaucracy; hint of missing tomorrow being connected to a scheduled inspection.
- **Exit beat:** new rooms open and the Visitor decides to investigate.

### Act II A: The peculiar residents (12–25 min)
- Kitchen: Mabel serves tea and claims the clocks are simply “resting.” Evidence on a sealed letter becomes visible after Impossible Tea puzzle.
- Library: Thimble's books reveal the house was created when forgotten dreams could not disappear; residents kept each other alive by sharing stories.
- Thimble's archive registry contains an exemption clause: documented residents + demonstrated care + functioning timekeeping count as a legitimate inhabited home.
- **Payoff setup:** The Auditor isn't allowed to erase a real recognized home without a hearing.

### Act II B: Mabel's secret (25–35 min)
- Greenhouse: Crumb is absurdly afraid of a root vegetable, but possesses a genuine promise note; the Moonflower remembers the caretaker's past actions and recently taken gear.
- Mabel confesses: “I stole tomorrow because they said our yesterdays did not count.” She admits inhabitants have started fading; she fears restoring clock brings inevitable closure.
- Fig and Thimble are upset with her but offer to help restore time while securing protection.
- Mirror Hallway reopens with a clue from the Auditor's own paperwork.
- **End of Act II:** dark edges of rooms seem too empty; NPCs aren't hurt, but their sprites momentarily desaturate/turn translucent (motion/flash reduced option).

### Act III: The hearing (35–50 min)
- Hallway reflection sequence opens clockroom; three gears/slots tell players the administrative exemption has exact proof requirements.
- Clock puzzle combines resident register, caretaking proof and tomorrow gear retrieved in Almost mode.
- Auditor enters. The Visitor, rather than attacking, offers clear evidence; Fig voluntarily signs as a witness, proving he is more than paperwork.
- The Auditor concedes protection is lawful, on condition that tomorrow moves again.
- Choice 1: OPEN DOORS. Choice 2: SECRET SANCTUARY. Both continue time and preserve the inhabitants.
- Epilogues show character changes, an understated gift-like feeling and a final small joke.

## Exemplar opening dialogue (short, production-ready starting point)

[Street / cat / first approach]
FIG: You are three minutes late.
VISITOR: I wasn't aware I had an appointment.
FIG: It's worse when they know.
VISITOR: Are you a cat wearing a tie?
FIG: I'm upper management. The tie is mandatory.
VISITOR: I seem to have received a letter about tomorrow.
FIG: Yes, very regrettable. We'd planned to have one.
VISITOR: Who is 'we'?
FIG: The residents. All of us. Including you, apparently.
VISITOR: I'm not a resident.
FIG: Then you should tell the Guestbook. It likes arguments.

[Inspect door]
VISITOR: The doorknob is shaped like a tiny hand.
FIG: Please don't shake it. It gets attached.

[Inspect guestbook before shift]
GUESTBOOK: DECLINED. YOU ARE NOT WHO YOU THINK YOU ARE.
VISITOR: Rude.
FIG: Efficient, though.

[Fig grants mirror]
FIG: A pocket mirror. It's not very good at showing what exists.
VISITOR: Why would I want that?
FIG: To find what nearly did.

[Inspect Almost portrait]
VISITOR: Someone carved a title under the frame: THE VISITOR.
FIG: Marvelous. The house has finally done some paperwork itself.

[Guestbook solved]
GUESTBOOK: ACCEPTED. WELCOME, THE VISITOR.
FIG: You see? Entirely normal hospitality.
VISITOR: The chandelier just bowed to me.
FIG: We do try.

[Erasure notice]
NOTICE: PROPERTY STATUS: UNFINISHED. CLOSURE: NEXT TOMORROW.
VISITOR: That's not reassuring.
FIG: It's much more reassuring than 'last yesterday.'

## NPC interaction samples and barks

**Miss Thimble**
THIMBLE: My books have gone out of order again. Events first, causes later. Very rude to chronology.
VISITOR: Have you tried shelving them by title?
THIMBLE: Titles are the one thing they refuse to keep.
...
THIMBLE: If you find a future on a lower shelf, please don't bend its spine.

**Sir Crumb**
CRUMB: HALT! Something orange lurks among the roots.
VISITOR: That's a carrot.
CRUMB: Your bravery borders on irresponsibility.
...
CRUMB: I fought seventeen battles. Sixteen were with a carrot. The seventeenth was a misunderstanding with a spoon.

**Mabel**
MABEL: Tea, dear? It doesn't solve problems. It gives them somewhere to sit.
...
VISITOR: You knew tomorrow wasn't lost.
MABEL: I did. I hid it. I thought one more night would be enough to save us.
VISITOR: The house is disappearing anyway.
MABEL: I know. I'm so sorry.

**Auditor**
AUDITOR: This structure is an unauthorized continuation of an uncompleted possibility.
VISITOR: It's a house. People live here.
AUDITOR: People have lived in filing errors before. The classification is still an error.
...
AUDITOR: I will inspect your evidence. Kindly refrain from growing additional rooms during the hearing.

## Final confrontation skeleton (branching with shared basis)

AUDITOR: Registry absent. Continuity suspended. Property to be removed.
VISITOR: The registry exists. Here are the residents.
THIMBLE: All properly catalogued. For once.
VISITOR: And proof they've taken care of one another.
CRUMB: I have guarded the greenhouse with my life. And several exaggerated stories.
FIG: As receptionist, I formally attest that we are a household.
AUDITOR: Your attestation is formatted correctly. Irritating.
MABEL: Restore the clock. Whatever happens, I won't keep them frozen again.
[PLAYER COMPLETES CLOCK]
AUDITOR: Timekeeping restored. This residence qualifies for Conditional Preservation. Choose designation.
[CHOICE: A — PUBLICLY ACCESSIBLE HOME / B — PRIVATE SANCTUARY]

### Ending A: Open Doors

Mansion appears every rainy Wednesday night; lost visitors occasionally find shelter. Fig hires himself as the only receptionist and attempts to charge admission in biscuits. Mabel hosts tea for strangers. The final visual has an open gate and warm windows as real dawn arrives.

### Ending B: Secret Sanctuary

The mansion becomes an official hidden refuge for unfinished lives. It can still be found by people who need it. Fig proudly files the exemption under `Excellent Administrative Work`; Mabel grows a tiny tomorrow flower. The final visual has an almost-visible gate at dawn.

### Ending completion note

Both endings must show dawn, time advancing and recognizable change in each major character. The final extra joke should lighten the goodbye without voiding the emotion. Credits follow, with replay-ending option and the opportunity to revisit solved mansion save if technically feasible.

## Writing standards

- Speech length usually <=2 short lines per bubble; long lore belongs in optional inspectables, not compulsory paragraphs.
- Use jokes arising from an NPC's priorities, not random memes, insults or constant fourth-wall-breaking.
- No comedic line should invalidate an emotional confession.
- Always offer one direct notebook clue after a riddling conversation.
- Keep dark tone more wistful than scary; no sexual violence, gore, jump scares or gratuitous cruelty.
- Don't use brand/movie lines, existing copyrighted dialogue or user-private exchanges without explicit authorization.
- Avoid 'perfect choice' morality. Either ending reflects a sensible value and is equally complete.
- If any dialogue in this file conflicts with playable clues, fix the writing with approval rather than secretly changing the puzzle design.
